# Method Notes

This scaffold models urban farming infrastructure as one distributed layer inside a broader food-system resilience architecture.

Simplified supply relationship:

\[
S_t = E_t + R_t + L_t + B_t - W_t
\]

where available supply \(S_t\) is external supply \(E_t\), regional supply \(R_t\), local urban production \(L_t\), buffer reserves \(B_t\), minus waste or losses \(W_t\).

Local redundancy ratio:

\[
\rho = \frac{L_t}{D_t}
\]

where \(L_t\) is local urban production and \(D_t\) is essential demand for the relevant food category.

Shock scenario:

\[
S_{shock} = (1-\alpha)E_t + R_t + L_t + B_t - W_t
\]

where \( \alpha \) is the fraction of external supply disrupted.

This scaffold uses simplified, synthetic calculations. Applied food-system resilience analysis requires local production data, household food-access data, procurement systems, distribution networks, crop-specific demand, seasonal variation, land tenure, water and energy data, food safety review, and community participation.
