# Urban Farming Infrastructure: Distributed Food Systems and Civic Resilience

This folder contains reproducible companion materials for the article **Urban Farming Infrastructure: Distributed Food Systems and Civic Resilience**.

The scaffold treats urban farming as a food-system resilience problem rather than a lifestyle or symbolic greening project. It uses synthetic data to evaluate local production, redundancy, supply-shock exposure, water and energy intensity, neighborhood food access, and distributed civic infrastructure.

## Article focus

Urban farming infrastructure cannot replace industrial agriculture or global food trade. Its resilience value lies in adding distributed production, proximity, traceability, local capacity, circular resource flows, and partial redundancy to otherwise centralized and fragile food systems.

## What this scaffold includes

- Synthetic urban farming and food-system resilience data.
- Local redundancy and buffer-capacity calculations.
- Supply-shock scenario analysis.
- Water, energy, and land-use efficiency indicators.
- Food-access and neighborhood resilience measures.
- Python and R workflows.
- SQL schema and analytical views.
- Julia, C, C++, Fortran, Go, and Rust examples for simple resilience calculations.
- Documentation for validation, ethics, and scope.
- A WordPress-ready GitHub embed block.

## Responsible use

This scaffold uses synthetic data and simplified assumptions. It is intended for public-interest learning, reproducible analysis, food-system resilience literacy, and transparent methodological demonstration.

It should not be used as the sole basis for zoning, public procurement, food emergency planning, land disposition, public-health policy, community investment, or infrastructure funding without local data validation, community participation, equity review, and accountable institutional decision-making.
